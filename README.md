# aSDMs
Development and Evaluation of aquatic Species Distribution Models (aSDMs)

Creator: Georgios Vagenas
Affiliation: Biogeography and Global Change Department, National Museum of Natural Sciences, CSIC, C/ Jose Gutierrez Abascal, 2, Madrid 28006, Spain

## Abstract:
Species Distribution Models (SDMs) have been traditionally developed for terrestrial and open-land systems, posing specific challenges when applied to aquatic ecosystems. We introduce a methodological framework that explores the applicability of high-resolution (~1 km) aquatic SDMs using presence-only data for freshwater organisms. Focusing on the Ichthyofauna of the Iberian Peninsula, we employed distinct modelling strategies to address niche truncation issues associated with endemic versus non-endemic and introduced species. Specifically, we applied global-to-regional models for non-endemic species and strictly regional models for endemic species. Our study systematically compares two modelling approaches for nowcasting ensemble aquatic SDMs: unconstrained models covering entire freshwater biogeographical regions where species are known to occur, and constrained models, limited to watersheds where species occur. Additionally, we incorporated the topology of hydroclimatic covariates in the aquatic SDMs, comparing conventional and hierarchical variable structures. The results demonstrate that the pre-constrained spatial modelling strategy proved to be the most effective approach. Moreover, climate predictors consistently outperformed hydrological covariates across all model configurations, as confirmed by three standard performance metrics (i.e., AUC, CBI, TSS). We conclude that all proposed modelling stages should be followed to accurately predict freshwater species distributions. This ensures comprehensive representation of spatial variability, appropriate covariate selection, and optimal model configuration—accounting for the unique complexity of aquatic ecosystems.

### Keywords: 
SDMs, freshwater ecosystems, fish, climate, hydrology
